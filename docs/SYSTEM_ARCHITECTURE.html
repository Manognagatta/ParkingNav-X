ðŸŽ¯Why (Design Reasoning)

Why Digital Twin (Not Just Simulator)?

Simulator: "If you do X, the result is Y." (Passive prediction)
Twin: "Here is the current state. Fork it. Try action A and see result Y1. Try action B and see result Y2. Then decide." (Active decision-support)
Difference: A Twin can be forked and compared. Member 3's optimizer uses this fork capability to test routing strategies before applying them.

Why Discrete-Event (Not Fixed Time Steps)?

Fixed time steps: Advance 1 second. Check for events. Do this 36,000 times per 10-hour day. Slow.
Discrete-event: Jump to the next event (only 1,000 events, not 36,000 time steps). Fast. Easier to explain in viva. Matches real campus reality (things happen in bursts, not smoothly).

Why Non-Homogeneous Poisson?

Homogeneous Poisson: Same arrival rate all day. Not realistic (no morning peak).
Non-homogeneous Poisson: Arrival rate changes by hour (morning peak, midday low, afternoon peak). Matches campus reality. Thinning algorithm is simple and reproducible.

Why BPR Formula for Roads?

Assumption: "Traffic congestion increases nonlinearly with flow."
Formula: t = t_free Ã— [1 + 0.15 Ã— (flow/capacity)^4]
Why? Standard in traffic engineering. When a road is 80% full, travel time doesn't doubleâ€”it increases exponentially. This is realistic.

Why Seed Discipline (Same Seed = Identical Results)?

Without: Run B1 with seed 42 â†’ B1 gets 100 vehicles. Run B2 with seed 42 â†’ B2 also gets 100 vehicles. Fair. But run B1 again â†’ Different 100 vehicles! Unfair comparison.
With: Same seed = same vehicles arrive at same times. B1 vs B2 differ only in strategy, not in random noise. Fair paired comparison.
Implementation: Master seed â†’ separate child streams for arrivals, dwell, service. Strategies don't see or control the seed.

Why 30 Seeds per Experiment?

1 seed: Lucky or unlucky. Could be outlier.
30 seeds: Statistical power. You can say "Strategy B is better than Strategy A with 95% confidence."
Practical: 30 Ã— 500 vehicles Ã— <10s = 2.5 minutes. Feasible.

Why Real Observations (E7)?

Simulator alone: "Based on assumptions, here's what would happen."
With real data: "Based on assumptions AND validated against reality, here's what would happen."
Honest: "Our simulator MAE is 5 vehicles. It overestimates occupancy by 3%. These are known limitations."
Impact: Makes your results credible to the review committee.

Why 85% Compliance?

Assumption: "Not everyone follows the routing recommendation."
Why? In real life, drivers have preferences, are impatient, or don't trust the system.
Modeling: 85% follow recommendation, 15% go to nearest preferred lot.
Impact: Shows your strategy works even with human unpredictability. No RL or AI neededâ€”just realistic behavior.

Why 133 Tests?

No tests: "It works on my machine." Not credible.
10 tests: You tested some pieces. Not comprehensive.
133 tests: Unit tests (config), state tests (invariants), simulation tests (conservation law), scenario tests (E1â€“E7), performance tests (1000 vehicles), real-data tests (calibration).
Impact: Bulletproof. Any change breaks a testâ€”you catch it immediately.
